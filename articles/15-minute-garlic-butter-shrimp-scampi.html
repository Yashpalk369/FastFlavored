Bottom Line Up Front (Quick Answer):

To make tender, restaurant-style garlic butter shrimp scampi in 15 minutes, sear seasoned shrimp for just 60 seconds per side in butter and olive oil, then transfer to a plate. Build the silky sauce in the same pan with minced garlic, dry white wine, and fresh lemon juice, swirling in 2 tablespoons of cold butter off the heat to create a glossy emulsion before tossing with al dente pasta.

Why This Scampi Beats Any Restaurant

  • Cooks in 15 Minutes: Shrimp cook faster than any other dinner protein—just 2 minutes total in the pan.
  • Emulsified Pan Sauce: Whisking cold butter into hot wine and starchy pasta water creates a rich sauce that clings to every noodle.
  • Pantry Friendly: Frozen peeled shrimp thaw in 7 minutes under cold running water, making this an ideal emergency dinner.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Cook Pasta: Boil linguine in well-salted water until al dente. Reserve 1/2 cup cooking water, then drain.
  2. Sear Shrimp: Melt 2 tbsp butter with olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Season shrimp with salt and pepper. Sear for 1 minute per side until pink and curled into a loose 'C'. Remove to a plate.
  3. Simmer Aromatics: Drop heat to medium. Sauté minced garlic and red pepper flakes for 45 seconds. Pour in white wine and lemon juice; simmer for 2 minutes.
  4. Emulsify Sauce: Turn heat to low. Whisk in the remaining 2 tbsp cold butter until glossy and emulsified.
  5. Combine: Toss in drained pasta, cooked shrimp with pan juices, and fresh parsley. Add 2-3 tbsp pasta water if needed to loosen sauce.
  6. Garnish: Finish with lemon zest and grated parmesan cheese. Serve hot!

Chef Elena’s 3 Scampi Rules

Swirl Cold Butter at the Very End: Professional Italian chefs always finish pan sauces by mounting with cold butter ("monter au beurre") off the heat. Cold butter forms a creamy emulsion that coats pasta without splitting into greasy oil!

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I make shrimp scampi without wine?
Yes! Simply substitute an equal amount of low-sodium chicken broth or vegetable broth, plus an extra teaspoon of freshly squeezed lemon juice to provide that essential bright acidity.
How do you prevent shrimp from getting rubbery in scampi?
Cook the shrimp for only 60 to 90 seconds per side until they just turn pink and form a loose 'C' shape, then remove them immediately from the hot skillet while building the pan sauce. Add them back only in the final 30 seconds to warm through.
