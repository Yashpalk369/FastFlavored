15-Minute Smoky Blackened Cajun Shrimp and Creamy Cheddar Grits

Plump Cajun spiced blackened shrimp served over velvety sharp white cheddar quick grits, drizzled with a rich garlic scallion pan sauce in 15 minutes.

Bottom Line Up Front (Quick Answer):

To make 15-minute blackened shrimp and grits, bring 2 cups chicken broth and 1 cup whole milk to a boil. Whisk in 3/4 cup quick-cooking grits (5-minute grits); reduce heat to low, cover, and cook for 5 minutes. Stir in 1 cup sharp white cheddar cheese and 2 tbsp butter. Meanwhile, toss 1 lb peeled shrimp with 1.5 tbsp Cajun seasoning. Sear in 2 tbsp sizzling butter in a cast-iron skillet over high heat for 2 minutes per side until charred. Spoon grits into bowls, top with blackened shrimp, and drizzle with skillet pan butter.

Why This Recipe Conquers Weeknights

  • Broth-Milk Grits Liquid Ratio: Cooking grits in equal parts chicken broth and whole milk provides deep savory richness and creamy smoothness in 5 minutes.
  • Cast-Iron High-Heat Blackening: High heat quickly chars the smoked paprika, garlic, and cayenne crust on shrimp without overcooking the delicate meat.
  • Brown Butter Pan Pan-Sauce: The residual butter, Cajun spices, and shrimp pan fond create an instant pan sauce to spoon over cheesy grits.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Cook Quick Cheddar Grits: In a medium saucepan, bring chicken broth and whole milk to a steady boil over medium-high heat. Slowly pour in quick grits in a thin stream while whisking continuously to prevent lumps. Reduce heat to low, cover with lid, and simmer gently for 5 minutes, stirring twice. Remove from heat and stir in 2 tbsp butter and shredded white cheddar until velvety and smooth. Cover to keep warm.
  2. Season Jumbo Shrimp: Pat peeled shrimp completely dry with paper towels. Toss thoroughly with 1.5 tbsp Cajun seasoning until heavily coated in aromatic spices.
  3. Sear to Smoky Blackened Perfection: Melt remaining 2 tbsp butter with a drop of oil in a cast-iron skillet over high heat until foaming. Add seasoned shrimp in a single flat layer. Sear undisturbed for 2 minutes until deeply blackened and crusty on the bottom, then flip and cook 90 seconds more until shrimp are opaque and pink.
  4. Make 30-Second Pan Sauce: Toss minced garlic and scallions into the hot pan drippings around the shrimp for 30 seconds, then squeeze fresh lemon juice over the shrimp. Swirl to create a glossy Cajun garlic butter sauce.
  5. Assemble & Serve: Spoon velvety white cheddar grits into wide bowls. Arrange blackened shrimp on top, drizzle sizzling garlic butter pan sauce generously over the bowl, and garnish with fresh chives and lemon slices!

Elena’s Chef Pro-Tip

Chef Secret: Pat your shrimp bone-dry before sprinkling with Cajun spices. Moisture on the shrimp causes them to steam instead of blackened, creating a soggy texture. Dry shrimp in smoking hot butter blacken instantly while staying tender and succulent inside.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use frozen thawed shrimp?
Yes! Thaw frozen shrimp completely in cold water, peel, and squeeze gently with clean paper towels to remove all water before seasoning.
What if my grits become too thick?
Grits thicken rapidly as they cool. Simply whisk in 2 to 3 tablespoons of warm chicken broth or milk right before plating to restore a luxurious, pourable consistency.
Is Cajun seasoning too salty?
Use a salt-free or low-sodium Cajun seasoning blend like Tony Chachere's No Salt or Slap Ya Mama Low-Sodium so you can control seasoning perfectly.
