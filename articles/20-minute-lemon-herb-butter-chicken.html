Bottom Line Up Front (Quick Answer):

To make 20-minute crispy lemon herb butter chicken, slice chicken breasts horizontally into thin cutlets and lightly dredge in flour. Sear in olive oil and 1 tablespoon butter for 3 minutes per side until golden. Deglaze the skillet with 1/2 cup chicken broth, fresh lemon juice, garlic, rosemary, and oregano, then swirl in 2 tablespoons cold butter off the heat to create a silky, glossy pan sauce.

Why Thin Cutlets Transform Chicken Dinners

  • Zero Dried-Out Centers: Thin 1/2-inch cutlets cook in just 6 minutes total, staying tender, juicy, and succulent.
  • Emulsified Pan Sauce: Whisking cold butter into hot lemon broth creates a glossy, clinging sauce that elevates every mouthful.
  • Fresh Herb Brightness: Rosemary and oregano give earthiness that cuts through the rich butter and bright citrus.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Season & Dredge: Season cutlets with salt and pepper. Lightly dredge in flour, shaking off all excess.
  2. Golden Sear: Heat oil and 1 tbsp butter in a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Sear cutlets for 3 to 4 minutes per side until golden. Remove to a platter.
  3. Deglaze Aromatics: Drop heat to medium. Sauté garlic and fresh herbs for 45 seconds. Pour in chicken broth and lemon juice, scraping browned bits from pan.
  4. Mount Butter: Simmer 2 minutes. Take pan off direct heat and swirl in remaining 2 tbsp cold butter until glossy and emulsified.
  5. Coat & Garnish: Return chicken to pan, coating with pan sauce. Garnish with lemon wheels and parsley. Serve immediately!

Elena’s Thin Cutlet Technique

Butterfly & Halve for Speed: Thick grocery store chicken breasts take 15-20 minutes to cook through, often leaving the outside stringy and dried out. Place your palm flat on top of the breast and slice horizontally through the center into two even cutlets. They sear rapidly in 6 minutes with maximum crust!

Frequently Asked Questions

Why dredge chicken cutlets in flour before searing?
A light dusting of flour creates a delicate golden crust that locks in moisture and prevents thin chicken breasts from drying out. As the pan sauce simmers, the flour clinging to the browned bits naturally thickens the sauce into a velvety glaze.
Can I make this recipe dairy-free?
Yes! Substitute the butter with a high-quality plant-based butter (such as Miyoko's or Earth Balance) to emulsify the lemon herb pan sauce without losing richness.
