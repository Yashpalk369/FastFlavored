Bottom Line Up Front (Quick Answer):

To make tender, caramelized honey garlic salmon in 20 minutes, roast 6-ounce salmon fillets and trimmed asparagus at 400°F (200°C) for 10-12 minutes, then broil on high for 90 seconds to blister the glaze. Line your sheet pan with foil to reduce cleanup time to under 60 seconds.

Why This Salmon Wins Every Time

  • Zero Stove Splatters: Everything bakes neatly inside the oven on a single rimmed baking sheet.
  • Natural Caramelization: Honey and soy sauce form an instant lacquer when hit with high heat broiler in the last 90 seconds.
  • Complete Protein & Veggie Combo: Fresh asparagus roasts concurrently in the same pan, absorbing the citrus butter juices.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Preheat: Set oven to 400°F (200°C). Line a large rimmed sheet pan with aluminum foil.
  2. Whisk Glaze: In a small bowl, whisk melted butter, honey, soy sauce, minced garlic, lemon juice, salt, and pepper until glossy.
  3. Assemble Sheet Pan: Place salmon skin-side down in the pan center. Scatter trimmed asparagus around fillets. Drizzle asparagus with olive oil. Spoon 3/4 of honey garlic glaze over salmon fillets. Tuck lemon slices between fillets.
  4. Bake: Bake at 400°F for 10-12 minutes until salmon is tender and flakes gently.
  5. Broil: Switch broiler to HIGH for 90 seconds until glaze bubbles and caramelizes.
  6. Finish: Drizzle remaining glaze over salmon, garnish with fresh parsley, and serve!

Chef Elena’s Secrets for Juicy Salmon

Never Overbake Salmon: Salmon continues to cook for 3-4 minutes after leaving the oven. Pull your sheet pan when the thickest part reads 135°F to 140°F on a digital thermometer. It will rest to a silky, melt-in-your-mouth 145°F!

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you know when sheet pan salmon is done cooking?
Salmon is perfectly cooked when it turns opaque pink and flakes gently when pressed with a fork. Using an instant-read thermometer, remove the salmon when it hits 135°F to 140°F (57-60°C); it will carry-over cook to 145°F while resting.
Can I use frozen salmon fillets?
Yes, but ensure the salmon is fully thawed first. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator, or submerge vacuum-sealed fillets in a bowl of cold tap water for 20 minutes before patting completely dry.
