Bottom Line Up Front (Quick Answer):

To make tender, smoky sheet-pan chicken fajitas in 25 minutes, slice chicken breast and tri-color bell peppers into uniform 1/2-inch strips. Toss directly on an 18x13-inch rimmed baking sheet with olive oil, fresh lime juice, chili powder, and cumin. Roast at 425°F for 15 minutes, then switch to high broil for 2 minutes to blister the pepper edges and achieve sizzling restaurant char.

Why Sheet-Pan Fajitas Rule Weeknights

  • Single-Pan Cleanup: Line your rimmed baking sheet with parchment paper or foil, and your dishwashing is practically finished before dinner starts.
  • Hands-Off Cooking: No standing over a spitting cast-iron skillet while flipping batches; the oven roasts everything evenly at once.
  • Customizable Family Dinner: Set out warm tortillas, guacamole, pico de gallo, and let everyone build their own tacos or low-carb bowls.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Preheat: Heat oven to 425°F (220°C). Line a large 18x13 rimmed sheet pan with parchment paper.
  2. Season: Place sliced chicken, bell peppers, and onion on the pan. Drizzle with olive oil and lime juice; sprinkle seasonings evenly over everything.
  3. Toss & Spread: Toss well with tongs until thoroughly coated. Spread across the pan in an even single layer without crowding.
  4. Roast: Bake for 14 minutes until chicken is cooked through (165°F internal).
  5. Broil for Char: Switch oven to high broil for 2 to 3 minutes until pepper edges are lightly blistered and charred.
  6. Serve: Squeeze extra lime juice over the pan. Serve immediately in warm tortillas with guacamole and salsa!

Elena’s High-Heat Broil Rule

Don't Skip the 2-Minute Broiler Finish: Traditional fajitas get their signature smoky flavor from blazing hot cast iron. By turning on your oven's broiler for the final 120 seconds, you replicate that irresistible restaurant smokiness and caramelized char right on your baking sheet!

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you get restaurant-style char on sheet pan fajitas?
Roast at high heat (425°F) on a preheated baking sheet, then turn on the oven's high broiler for the last 2 to 3 minutes. The direct top heat blisters the pepper skins and caramelizes the edges of the chicken perfectly.
Can I use chicken thighs instead of breasts for fajitas?
Yes! Boneless skinless chicken thighs work extraordinarily well. Thighs stay extra juicy and forgiving under the high roasting heat without drying out.
